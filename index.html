Substrate and Surface Terms

Substrate. The base material beneath the tiles: existing walls or floors that may be tiled, screeded, sheeted or already damaged. The parent guide says a better enquiry records the room, age and visible symptoms because the finished surface depends on the condition of the base.

Screed to falls. The sloped layer that directs water toward the waste. Falls must be planned with the drainage set-out before the membrane goes down, since they affect threshold heights and finished floor levels.

Substrate preparation. The rectification work needed before waterproofing or tiling starts. Older villa substrates through Norwood and Unley, newer slabs around Mawson Lakes or Mount Barker, and apartment access in the city can each change preparation and sequencing, so they should trigger inspection questions rather than cost assumptions.

Waterproofing and Drainage Terms

Wet-area membrane. The continuous layer that actually stops water. The parent guide is explicit that tiles and grout are finishes, not the waterproof layer, and that membrane detailing must be confirmed in writing. Where waterproofing or screeding is required, it should be separately described in a quote, not buried inside a general line item.

Junction treatment. Detailing at the joins around fixtures, walls and floors. Penetrations are the holes where pipes and fittings pass through the membrane; both are common starting points for moisture symptoms.

Regrouting. Replacing grout between tiles. The parent guide states regrouting may suit a maintenance issue, but should not be presented as a universal leak repair. If a shower is already leaking, ask what has been diagnosed before regrouting is proposed, because moisture symptoms can start with sealant, plumbing, substrate movement or the membrane system.

Layout and Finish Terms

Set-out. The planned positions of tiles, niches, trims and transitions. Large-format tiles, niches and cabinetry lines all affect it, and adjoining floor transitions need to be settled with the layout rather than after it.

Movement joints. Planned gaps that absorb substrate movement. They need to be designed with the substrate and layout in mind, which is why the parent guide classes them as scope questions, not decoration details.

Grout and trims. The visible finishing lines. Grout preference, trim profiles and niche details should be recorded before layout is finalised, because changing them late can disturb falls, thresholds and membrane boundaries that are already locked in.

Standards, Licensing and Handover

The supplied research context points to Consumer and Business Services South Australia, the National Construction Code and AS 3740 for wet-area work. This glossary does not restate technical rules, because the exact application depends on the project and the responsible trades. It is enough to ask which standard, product specification and licence scope a provider is relying on.

The parent guide notes that waterproofing and tiling may sit within one coordinated scope where the contractor holds the appropriate South Australian authorisation, and that the provider remains responsible for confirming scope, licensing, products and trade coordination in writing. Handover sequencing is the final term: the point where waterproofing is signed off before tiling begins, protecting both stages of the bathroom tiling adelaide reader's written scope from finish-led quotes that skip the base work.

  1. Record the room. Note the age, existing surface, visible symptoms, previous repairs and water exposure for the shower, ensuite or splash zone before requesting quotes.
  2. Read the scope line by line. Check preparation, tile format, layout, trims, grout, movement joints, access and waste are each identified rather than lumped together.
  3. Separate waterproofing from finishes. Confirm screeding, membrane preparation, junction treatment, penetrations and thresholds are described in their own items.
  4. Question leak diagnoses. If a shower leaks, ask what has been diagnosed before accepting regrouting as the proposed fix.
  5. Confirm responsibility in writing. Ask which standard, product specification and licence scope the provider relies on, and keep the written record with the quote.
